## High-level summary
FlameMap is an SI-powered briefing on NASA's microgravity fire experiments for crewed missions. You choose where the crew will live (station cabin, lunar habitat, rover, Mars habitat, spacesuit, NASA's Exploration Atmosphere or your own values) and what you are working on (prevention, detection or suppression). An AI briefing explains what the closest of 19 flight investigations from NASA's Physical Sciences Informatics (PSI) database found and what has never been tested, with a citation on every sentence, and you can ask your own questions in plain words. Every AI sentence was checked against the data by a script and reviewed by a person before publication, so the site needs no API key or server. Behind the briefing, a transparent ranking shows the arithmetic for every match score.

## Project details
- **What it does.** A Briefing home page with the AI briefing for each environment and task (closest evidence, what it found, what was never tested, what to read next), the computed gaps, the closest studies and suggested questions; "Ask the evidence", a conversation over 66 checked, cited answers that understands paraphrased questions and suggests follow-ups; a "How the AI works" page with the pipeline and live check counts; a Rank page with the per-factor math; an evidence map of 875 runs by oxygen and pressure with the chosen environment marked; key findings on every study, each with its NASA source quote; an explorer whose study pages open with their checked AI answer and "Studies like this one"; side-by-side comparison; CSV and JSON export.
- **How it works.** We read the PSI records and their experimental tables, converted units, and stored every condition with its source and a confidence label. The ranking is transparent arithmetic: distance from the environment to the tested range for each factor, combined with adjustable weights. A condition a study did not record counts as untested, so missing data never raises a score; for experiments in station cabin air with no oxygen or pressure column, cabin air is assumed and labeled.
- **How the AI is kept honest.** AI writes the findings, briefings, answers and related-study reasons at build time, from the PSI record text, NASA's Researcher's Guide and the computed rankings and gaps. A script fails the build if any quote is not word for word in its source, any number is not in what the sentence cites, any study is named without being cited, or any study called "closest evidence" is not in the computed top five; the current text passes with 295 sentences, 667 citations and 311 numbers checked. A team member reviewed every briefing, answer and related-study reason against its sources and signed off on a hash of the text, so any later change fails the build until it is reviewed again. The app labels all AI text and says whether a person has reviewed it.
- **Why no live chatbot.** Writing the text ahead of time means every sentence is checked before anyone reads it, and the site needs no API key, server or per-visit cost. Visitors' questions are matched to the checked answers by meaning, with a small open sentence-embedding model (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) running in the browser from the site's own files. It compares questions; it does not write text. The answer layer is built so a live mode (a visitor's own key or a hosted proxy, with the same checks in the browser) can be added later.
- **What we found for a lunar habitat (30% O2, 70.3 kPa, 0.166 g).** The highest oxygen level in any solid-fuel data table is 22.2% (BASS-II). 37 runs reach 30% oxygen or more at 75 kPa or less, all with gas or liquid-droplet fuels. No solid-fuel table records pressure. None of the 19 investigations ran at partial gravity. The one direct hint about higher oxygen comes from ACME BRE, whose record reports that emulated material flames kept burning without airflow in elevated-oxygen atmospheres but went out at 21% oxygen; NASA also states its 1-g materials test is not conservative in microgravity.
- **Limits.** It is a research-navigation aid. It does not predict fire behavior or certify safety.
- **Tools.** TypeScript, React, Vite, Recharts, Node.js, Vitest, Transformers.js with the all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embedding model (in-browser semantic search), Claude Code (AI assistant).

## How it addresses the challenge
| Challenge ask | Feature |
|---|---|
| Summarize investigations | Explorer and detail panels |
| Rank by relevance to crewed environments | Rank page |

## NASA data and resources
PSI Data Repository, combustion science (https://psi.nasa.gov): records PSI-10, 20, 21, 22, 23, 25, 26, 39, 47, 68, 69, 98, 99, 100, 101, 102, 106, 107, 159 (ranked) and PSI-60, 62, 115, 117, 142 (linked modeling studies). We used the record descriptions and the "Experimental table" files, and NASA's "A Researcher's Guide to: ISS Combustion Science" (2015) for findings, cited by page. DOIs are listed on the app's Method and sources page. Also linked: NASA spin-off pages and NASA history pages from the challenge Resources tab.

## Use of artificial intelligence
We used an AI assistant (Anthropic Claude, Claude Opus 5.5 through Claude Code) to help plan the project, write the code, read PSI record text to fill in descriptive fields, draft the short summaries, and write the key findings, the environment briefings, the Ask the evidence answers and their paraphrases, and the related-study reasons. All of this text is generated before the site is built, only from the PSI records, NASA's Researcher's Guide and the computed data, and a script checks every quote, number and citation against those sources. A team member reviewed the briefings, answers, paraphrases and related-study reasons and signed off on the exact text (`docs/AI_TEXT_REVIEW.md`, `pipeline/ai/meta.json`); the short study summaries have not yet been reviewed and are labeled as AI-drafted. In the browser, an open sentence-embedding model (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) only matches questions to the checked answers; no AI service is called at runtime. All AI text is labeled in the app. The ranking uses arithmetic, not an AI model. No AI-generated images or video are used.
